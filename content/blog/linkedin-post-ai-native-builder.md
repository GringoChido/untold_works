# LinkedIn Post

---

I've been building a digital agency powered by AI for about a year now.

I still have no idea what to call myself.

A year ago I was making graphics in Canva. Today? I'm writing database queries in Supabase, scraping the web with Apify, building MCP servers, deploying full-stack apps on Vercel, running headless browser automations with Puppeteer, and building dashboards connected to Salesforce.

I didn't go to school for any of this. A year ago I didn't even know what half these platforms were.

And somehow I'm using them every day. And actually understanding them. That's the part that still blows my mind.

Here's what a random Monday looked like last month:

→ Check a client's GA4 data
→ Tweak their Google Ads bidding
→ Build a service page in Next.js and TypeScript
→ Write ad copy in English and Spanish
→ Set up a Supabase backend for a client portal
→ Scrape competitor pricing with Apify
→ Automate lead notifications with Claude Code
→ Deploy everything before dinner

That's not one job. That's like six. It used to take a whole team.

I'm not saying that to flex. I'm saying it because it surprised me too. The tools just changed everything. AI didn't replace my thinking. It removed the friction between thinking and doing. I have an idea at 9am and it's live by lunch. That was not possible two years ago.

The wildest part? I'm working daily with platforms I'd never even heard of before this year. Supabase. Apify. Puppeteer. Salesforce APIs. Netlify Edge Functions. I would've been intimidated by any one of these 18 months ago. Now they're just part of the Tuesday workflow.

That's what nobody tells you about building with AI. It doesn't just speed up what you already know. It makes you capable of things you never thought you'd understand.

I've been watching what other people in this space call themselves:

"AI-Augmented Creative Technologist." Accurate but nobody's remembering that at a party.
"One-Person Agency Operator." Probably the most honest one.
"Vibe Builder." Fun, but try putting that on an invoice.

None are perfect. But they're all closer than "web developer" or "digital marketer." Those titles describe one lane. What's actually happening is you're moving across all of them because the cost of switching contexts dropped to almost nothing.

So if people ask "wait, so you're a developer?" and your answer is "kind of, but also not really"... just know you're not alone.

The role doesn't have a name yet because we're still figuring it out.

And honestly? That's the fun part.
