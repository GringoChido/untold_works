## Headline
Why You're Paying $15,000/Year for Tools You'll Never Own

## Sections

### 1. The Subscription Economy Was Designed to Extract Maximum Value From You
- Brief history: software used to be purchased, now it's rented
- The shift benefited vendors, not customers
- "Recurring revenue" is Wall Street's favorite metric — it's your money

### 2. The Math People Avoid
- Average small business SaaS spend: $9,000–$15,000/year
- Over 5 years: $45,000–$75,000
- Over 10 years: $90,000–$150,000+
- And you own nothing at the end

### 3. What "Ownership" Actually Means
- You get the source code
- You get the data
- You get the credentials
- You can hire anyone to maintain it
- No vendor can take it away or raise prices

### 4. The Objections (and Why They Don't Hold Up)
- "But SaaS tools get updates" → Most updates are irrelevant or paid upsells
- "Custom is too expensive" → Only if you compare month 1. Compare year 3.
- "What about maintenance?" → Hosting costs $0–$20/mo. Updates are optional.
- "I don't have a dev team" → We train your existing team. No dev needed.

### 5. The Question You Should Be Asking
- Not "which SaaS tool should I use?" but "why am I renting at all?"
- The subscription model is so normalized we forgot to question it
